Each story: AI draft, rule checks, a second AI as critic, then shown. Rejected drafts, rewrites and safe fallbacks are all counted.

Students can report a story; the teacher can hide or restore any of them.

Why it matters: responsible AI a school can check

Real screen (Stories and AI tab) · counts include simulated class data

The last teacher feature is about trust. Schools are rightly nervous about AI writing for children. So the Stories and AI tab shows the whole pipeline: how many stories the AI drafted, how many the rule checks sent back, how many the second AI rejected as critic, how many were shown, and how many fell back to the prepared story. Below it is a decision log, story by story. Students can report a story they think is wrong, and the teacher can hide or restore any story. This is what responsible AI looks like in a classroom: not a promise, but a page the teacher can check.
