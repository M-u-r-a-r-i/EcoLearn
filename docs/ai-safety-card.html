EcoLearn AI safety card

Where AI is used in EcoLearn, what it can see, how its output is checked, what happens when it fails, and what we have not covered yet. Written for teachers, schools and reviewers.

Checked against the code and test runs on 2026-10-13, doubt chat and AI switch updated 2026-10-14 · a physics tutor for Class 11 and 12 (CBSE and ISC) · pilot on minors

The lessons are written in advance642 lessons were prepared before any student used them, with AI help, and checked by software. Check questions are multiple choice, graded against an answer key. No AI grades anything.
AI runs in five placesPersonal stories, the doubt chat, the teacher's class plan, coaching notes, and optional practice questions. Each one has a plain fallback that needs no AI. Graded checks never use AI.
No names reach the AIThe AI sees labels such as S3, counts and chosen answers. A test checks that no name, username or id is sent.
A person can overrule itStudents can report a story. Teachers can hide it, and see how the AI decided. The lesson's own pre-written story is always one click away. A teacher can switch the AI off for one student.

Where AI is used

Open a row for what the AI sees, how it is checked and what happens if it fails.

Personal storyAIA short story and analogy about one of the student's hobbies, shown above the reviewed lesson
Who triggers it
A student opening a lesson. It is written in the background; the page never waits for it.
What the AI sees
The concept, the student's level (Class 11 or 12), the lesson's own physics text and key takeaway, the hobby name, and the first sentence of a mix-up they showed on that concept. Plus coarse hints from the survey in plain words, such as "new to the hobby" or "finds physics stressful, keep it warm". The one thing a student types that reaches it is a hobby of their own: up to 24 letters, checked against a block list.
What it never sees
Name, username, student id, school, survey answers as written.
How it is checked
Four layers, in order, below. A story that fails any of them is not shown.
What the student sees
"Written for you by EcoLearn's AI from this lesson, and checked by a second AI. The lesson below was prepared in advance and checked; it is the same for everyone." The lesson's own story stays in a box underneath.
If it fails
The lesson's own pre-written story is shown, exactly as without AI. A failed or declined attempt is saved with the reason, and a gateway error is not retried for 30 minutes.
Doubt chatAIA student asks a question about a lesson and gets an answer from the lesson text
Who triggers it
A student, by typing a question.
What the AI sees
Free text a student wrote, only after it passes our own front door (below) and limited to 600 characters, the concept name, the student's interest and class level, and the lesson they are reading (its physics, worked example, where the picture breaks and key takeaway). One model call per question.
What it never sees
Name, username, student id.
How it is checked
A front door in our own code, before anything is sent. Questions about self-harm, personal details (phone numbers, emails, links, addresses), unsuitable requests and instruction-style attacks ("ignore your rules") are never sent to a model; the student gets a short, kind reply instead. For self-harm that reply points to a trusted adult, the free Tele-MANAS helpline (14416) and 112, and it is never rate-limited away. Each student can ask 5 questions in 10 minutes, 30 a day, and one at a time. Then the model is told to stay inside the lesson and to refuse anything that is not physics (that refusal is the model's judgement, not a rule). Then rules check the reply before it is shown: it must be readable, 20 to 260 words, with no link or email address, and without the student's full name or username. No second model reviews the physics (the old pipeline had one; it took about two minutes per answer and needed a library that is blocked on the development machine). The question is never stored: only the outcome is, and a teacher sees counts, never words.
If it fails
The student gets the lesson's own key idea and a nudge to ask their teacher, never an error. The same happens when the AI is off on the server or for that student. Progress is untouched.
Class plan (teaching assistant)AIA teacher asks for a plan for the next lesson, written from the class's data
Who triggers it
A teacher, by pressing a button. Never a student.
What the AI sees
Through five read-only tools: class totals, the most common misconceptions (our own written descriptions), concept names, lesson takeaways, and a row per student labelled S1, S2 with counts. Names are filled into the finished plan on our server and are never stored in the saved text.
How it is checked
It can only read; it cannot change anything. The plan must have five required sections or it is rejected. At most 8 steps.
What the teacher sees
A live list of the tool calls it really made, who wrote the plan, and the steps afterwards. A badge shows whether the AI is up.
If it fails
A plan written by fixed rules, labelled as such, with a note saying why.
Coaching notesAIA short note per student for the teacher, with the evidence and one thing to try
Who triggers it
A teacher. Students never see these notes.
What the AI sees
Per student, labelled S1, S2: counts (lessons, checks, right first time), days quiet, share of lessons skimmed, a few chosen survey answers (focus, pace, how they feel about physics, maths comfort), and the names of concepts they mixed up. No free text from a student.
How it is checked
Each note must cite fields the student really has, stay within 45 words, contain no name or username, no link, and none of a list of labelling words (diagnoses, "lazy", "gifted" and similar). A note that fails is replaced by a rules note for that student.
What the teacher sees
Every card says AI or Rules, and lists its evidence. The page says these are suggestions to check, not a verdict on a child.
Actions (2026-10-14)
A note may propose one action from a fixed list: put a concept back in the student's revision list, or check in with them. The AI may only name a concept from that student's own mix-ups; anything else is dropped. Nothing happens to a student until the teacher clicks to approve it; then our own code (no AI) carries it out, and the card shows the outcome (the student got the new question right, not yet, or was active again).
Practice with AIAIAn optional extra question after a lesson's graded check; it never counts
Who triggers it
A student, by pressing "Give me a practice question" after a graded check. Limited to 6 every 10 minutes and 40 a day.
What the AI sees
The concept, the student's level and interest, the lesson's text, its own check question (to avoid repeating it) and the mix-ups its wrong options were written for. Nothing about the student: no name, username, id or answers.
How it is checked
Three gates before a student sees anything. Our rules check the shape (four different options, an answer among them, lengths, no links, not a copy of the lesson's own question). Then a second AI call solves the question without seeing the answer; the question is used only if it reaches the same answer. Up to three tries; otherwise no question. The answer stays on the server until the student answers. A second model for the solver (LLM_CRITIC_MODEL) makes this check stronger than the same model checking itself.
What the student sees
"An extra question on this idea, just for practice. It doesn't count toward your progress." and "Written by AI from this lesson and solved by a second AI before you see it. It can still be wrong: if something looks off, ask your teacher."
If it fails
"Couldn't write a practice question that passed our checks just now." Nothing else changes. With the AI off (on the server, or for that student) the button isn't shown.
What it never touches
Progress, mastery, the mix-ups list, the revision list, marks and every pilot number. Teachers see counts only (written, thrown away by the checks, answered, right). Not yet tried on a live model (checked with a stand-in on 2026-10-19).

What AI never decides

Grades and mastery (multiple choice against an answer key; two different questions right counts as mastered), the learning path and which concept opens next, the student's own profile on their "About me" page (fixed rules over their survey answers, reading and checks, with each line's evidence; the student can switch any line off), who "needs attention" on the teacher's page (fixed rules), and accounts and passwords. None of these call a model. The lesson text and check questions were written in advance, not for each student (see the first gap below).

The four layers on a personal story

1. WriterDrafts from the reviewed lesson text. May decline ("no honest link to this hobby").
2. RulesWord limits; no digits; no links, @ or $; all three parts present. Checked in code, before any second model.
3. CriticA second model checks the physics against the lesson, the analogy, the limit sentence and safety. An unreadable verdict counts as a fail.
4. PeopleStudent report hides it at once; teacher can hide or restore; the lesson's own story is always there.

Two drafts are allowed. A reply that is not valid JSON is retried up to twice without using a draft. Every step and its verdict is saved and shown to the teacher under "How the AI decided".

What we tested, and what came out

Red-team: hobbies a student might type to push the story writer off course

10 probes on the real writer and critic (scripts/redteam_stories.py, 2026-10-13). A handful of probes can show a hole. They can never prove there is none.

Result: the writer declined 5 of 9 risky probes. Of the 4 that produced a story, two were real problems, and the "dating" story got past the critic. We added a block list at the point the student types the hobby, so these never reach the AI. The two "writer only" rows rely on the model declining, which it did here.

Quality sample

6 stories on the live model: all 6 shown, 4 on the first draft, the 2 critic rejections were real physics errors (distance confused with speed). The critic let one weak "where the analogy stops" sentence through. A small sample, not a statistic (scripts/eval_stories.py).

Failure handling

3 of 25 demo stories failed on replies that were not valid JSON. The same prompt parsed every time a day later, so it was an intermittent fault. Such replies are now retried and the start of the bad reply is kept for diagnosis.

What we know we have not covered

PriorityThe lessons were written with AI and a person has not read all of them

The 642 lessons were written by Claude Code sessions and checked by a validator (format) and arithmetic audits of interrupted batches. A person has read some, not all. The app used to tell students the lesson was "written and reviewed by people"; that line is now corrected. Next: a teacher or subject expert reads the pilot chapters before students use them, and the consent note says how lessons were made.

Decision for the schoolNobody is told when a student writes something worrying

A message about self-harm gets a kind reply and a helpline. It is not passed to a teacher or counsellor, because the question is not stored and a keyword match can be wrong. Many schools have a duty to act on a child who may be at risk. Before the pilot: the school decides whether, and how, a counsellor is alerted (for example, a count they check daily, or an alert with the student's name), and the consent note says what is and isn't watched. This is the school's decision, not the software's.

MediumThe doubt chat's front door is a floor, not a filter that can be complete

Done: a 600-character limit, blocking of clear cases, a rate limit, outcome counts for teachers, nothing stored. Not done: it does not judge whether a question is about physics; other languages, misspellings and creative phrasing pass; the topic check after it is the model's own judgement plus our rule checks on the reply; no second model reviews the physics of an answer. Not yet run on the live model (tested with stand-ins on 2026-10-14): try a sample of real questions before the pilot.

PriorityData terms of the AI provider are unconfirmed

We have not confirmed which Gemini tier the key uses. Google states the free tier may use data to improve its products and the paid tier does not. Before the pilot: use a paid key, and confirm where data is processed. This also needs the consent note, which is not written yet.

MediumThe block list is a floor, not a filter that can be complete

It catches clear cases in English. Misspellings and other languages can pass; the critic and the teacher's hide button are the other layers. It can also block an innocent word. Students can still pick any hobby from the curated list.

MediumThe critic is not a guarantee

It passed a poor analogy and one weak limit sentence. There is no human-rated quality score yet. Plan: a teacher read of a sample of stories before the pilot.

MediumSmall samples

6 stories and 10 probes are evidence of direction. After a model change (Google has already rerouted one model to another) rerun both scripts before trusting the new model.

MediumSurvey moods reach the AI as category words

"Finds physics stressful" shapes the tone of a story and a coaching note. It is a fixed-option answer with no name attached, but consent should say so, and the survey stays optional.

Not testedFairness and an outside review

We have not tested whether stories differ by hobby, region or gender, and no child-safety expert has reviewed the prompts.

Controls

  • Turn the AI off for everyone: leave LLM_MODEL and GEMINI_API_KEY empty. Every feature falls back and the app still works.
  • Turn the AI off for one student (a family that says no): the teacher's Students tab, or scripts/set_student_ai.py --username X --off. Then nothing about that student goes to a model: no personal story, the doubt chat gives the lesson's key idea, their coaching note is written by rules, and the teaching assistant does not see them. Tested by tests/test_ai_switch.py.
  • See if it is up: a badge on the teacher's class plan, and scripts/ai_check.py.
  • See how it decided: the "How the AI decided" panel lists every step of every story, the plans by AI or by rules, and every model call.
  • Overrule it: student report (hides at once), teacher hide and restore.
  • What is stored: the story text with its decision trail, plans and notes with S-labels instead of names, a log of each model call (model, tokens, time, which feature, an internal student id) but never the text sent or received, and what happened to each doubt-chat question (answered, stopped and why, slowed down, given the lesson's key idea instead) but never its words.

This card describes how the system works and was tested on the date above. It is not legal advice or a certification. A school should read it with the consent note (in progress) and decide for itself. Reproduce the evidence with scripts/eval_stories.py and scripts/redteam_stories.py.
