Step two: a five-minute starting check. Eight multiple-choice questions, no marks, and he can say “I’m not sure yet” instead of guessing. The important design choice: every wrong option was written to catch one specific misconception. So when Aarav picks v equals u plus a t squared as consistent, we don’t just know he got it wrong, we know which idea he has mixed up. This is graded by rules, with no AI, so the result is the same for every student and can be compared.
