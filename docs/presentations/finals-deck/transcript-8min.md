## PRESENTER A

### Slide 1 · Title (0:00)
Good morning. We are team EcoLearn, under Professor M C Chinnaiah. I'm *[name]*, with *[name]*.
EcoLearn is an AI tutor that teaches every concept through something the student already loves. We
built it for Class 11 and 12 physics, but **physics is only our example**: the same software works for
any subject. These pictures are one law, Newton's, taught through five different interests.

### Slide 2 · Learning today (0:30)
More and more learning happens alone: homework, videos, apps. That builds independence, but four things
go wrong: motivation fades, the content is one-size-fits-all, mistakes go unseen, and feedback comes too
late, at the exam.

### Slide 3 · Problem statement (0:55)
So our problem: *"Generic learning ignores individual interests and ways of understanding, leading to
disengagement and reduced motivation."*

### Slide 4 · Our solution (1:10)
EcoLearn fits the content to the learner. It **gets to know them**: interests, hobbies, how they learn,
and a five-minute check. It **teaches through their world**: cricket, football, gaming, phones or
motorsport, plus a story about their own hobby. It **finds the exact mix-up** behind a wrong answer. And
it **keeps them going** with practice and revision. The teacher stays in the loop. Today you'll see
physics; swap in another syllabus and the same four steps work.

### Slide 5 · How it works (1:45)
Behind the scenes, EcoLearn works in four steps. One, **retrieve**: the syllabus concepts, CBSE and ISC
for this demo, are structured in advance, and we pull the student's own lesson and profile. Two,
**generate**: a story-writer AI drafts a short story around the student's own interest. Three, **review**:
a second AI acting as a critic, plus fixed safety rules, checks the physics and where the analogy breaks
before the student sees it. If a draft fails, they get the prepared story instead. Four, **deliver**: the
student gets their story, a worked example and check questions, marked instantly by rules.

### Slide 6 · Our agent system (2:15)
We are clear about what is AI. For students: a story writer with a critic, a doubt chat, and practice
questions. For teachers: a lesson planner and a coaching agent. But everything that **judges** a student,
marks, mastery, the learning path, is **rules, on purpose**. No AI marks anyone, and every AI part has a
fallback.

### Slide 8 · Meet the student (2:45)
Let's follow one student, from a sample class we built. Aarav, Class 11. He *dreads* physics, his maths is
shaky, he learns by trying an example first, and he loves cricket and badminton. These are his own
answers when he first signed in.

### Slide 10 · Step 1, sign in (3:05)
His teacher made his account: username and password, **no email, no phone**, because these are minors.
Four minutes of taps about *him*, not physics, and every question is skippable.

### Slide 11 · Step 2, starting check (3:20)
Then eight questions, no marks. **Every wrong option catches one specific misconception**, so a wrong
answer tells us *which idea* he has mixed up.

### Slide 12 · Step 3, his path (3:35)
That becomes his own map: what he knows is done, his two mix-ups go first.

**Handoff:** *[B's name]* will show what happens when he starts learning.

## PRESENTER B

### Slide 13 · Step 4, the lesson (3:50)
His lesson is a prepared cricket lesson, with "try an example" first, because that's how he learns. On
top, an AI writes a short story around his hobby, badminton, and a second AI checks it before he sees it.
If the AI fails, he gets the prepared story. Nothing breaks.

### Slide 14 · Step 5, quick check (4:15)
Then one question, and he says how sure he is. **Sure but wrong** is a firmly held mix-up, worth a
teacher's minute.

### Slide 15 · Step 6, a wrong answer (4:30)
He picks 15 metres, sure. EcoLearn names the slip: he subtracted the way back, that's displacement, not
distance. The mix-up goes on his list and comes back for revision.

### Slide 16 · Step 7, stuck? Ask (4:45)
He asks in his own words, and a real AI answer comes back, grounded in his lesson and in cricket. A
safety layer screens every question before any AI sees it.

### Slide 18 · Step 9, about me (5:00)
And he sees what EcoLearn thinks of him, with the evidence. If it's wrong, one tap on "That's not me"
switches it off. No silent profiling.

### Slide 19 · For the teacher (5:15)
For the teacher, this is a teaching assistant, not another dashboard. A class is set up in two minutes,
and the AI never sees a student's name.

### Slide 20 · The same evening (5:30)
The class page shows who needs attention, found by rules. Then an AI agent reads what the class actually
did and writes a ten-minute starter for tomorrow. This is a real plan.

### Slide 22 · The mix-up map (5:45)
Because every wrong option maps to a misconception, this reads like a **diagnosis, not a mark sheet**.

### Slide 23 · Coaching notes (5:55)
For each student, one suggestion with evidence, and it's a button. The teacher approves; EcoLearn does it.

### Slide 24 · How the AI decided (6:05)
And the teacher can see every AI step and hide any story. Responsible AI is a page you can check.

### Slide 25 · What it measures (6:15)
Here is what a school gets, on a **simulated** class of 25, every number computed by the real product:
who is active, lessons opened, 79 percent right first time, 78 percent said it helped. Our real pilot is
a randomised comparison, fixed before it starts.

### Slide 28 · What a score hides (6:35)
A score says "wrong". EcoLearn says which idea, who, and how sure they were.

### Slide 29 · Technology readiness (6:45)
It runs in any browser, phone or laptop. Every AI part has a fallback, and changing the AI provider is a
setting. The full student and teacher journey works today, with automated tests. Next: a school pilot.

### Slide 30 · Commercial viability (7:10)
Lessons are made once and shared, so AI adds only a few rupees per student a month. Schools pay per
student or for the teacher dashboard. And it scales cheaply: **a new subject, board or language is new
content, not new software.** Physics is our first subject, not our only one.

### Slide 31 · Thank you (7:45)
Thank you. EcoLearn: learning that speaks each learner's language. We'd love your questions.
