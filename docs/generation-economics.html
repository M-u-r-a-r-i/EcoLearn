Commercial viability · EcoLearn

What if we generated the whole syllabus for every student?

The cost of writing every physics lesson fresh for each student in their own interest, compared with EcoLearn’s shared lesson library, and what changes as the number of students grows.

Prepared 28 October 2026. Prices: Google’s published paid-tier list prices, checked 13 October 2026, at ₹88 per US dollar. Figures marked Measured come from our own runs; Assumed are our inputs; Illustrative show a mechanism, not a measurement.

The short answer

  • Per student, it costs ₹103 to ₹6,086, once, for the two years of Class 11 and 12. On the model we use today, that is ₹332 for one explanation per concept, or ₹996 for three lesson formats.
  • EcoLearn’s library costs ₹1,551 to ₹30,428 in total, once, no matter how many students use it. Each student then adds about ₹91 over two years for the live AI extras (their stories, chat, practice and the teacher’s tools).
  • As students grow, the gap grows in a straight line. At a million students, per-student generation costs ₹42 crore (cheap model) to ₹618 crore (strong model, three formats), against about ₹9 crore for EcoLearn.
  • Money is not what breaks first. At cheap-model prices it is affordable. What does not scale is checking: every student would read lessons no person and no shared fix has ever touched. Waiting time, model capacity and fair assessment break next.

The rule of thumb. A library costs (concepts × interests × formats) lessons, once. Per-student generation costs (concepts × formats) lessons for every student. So as soon as you have more students than interests, the library is cheaper. With 5 interests, that happens at the sixth student.

5 · What breaks before the money does

The real limits of writing everything per student

No one can check it

Each student gets lessons nobody else has seen, so a teacher can never review them and a fix helps only one student. In a library, a wrong answer key is fixed once for everyone. Illustrative If 1 lesson in 200 slipped past the AI checks, each student would meet about one wrong lesson, and a million students would hold a million different ones.

Students would wait

In our live trial, the free model took 65 to 226 seconds to write one short story, and often timed out. A full lesson is longer. A whole syllabus is 249 lessons, so it must be written ahead of time, and a new student would wait hours to start. Measured

Capacity runs out

About a billion model calls for a million students, all competing for the same provider limits. A library needs a few thousand calls, once.

Learning cannot be measured fairly

To know whether EcoLearn works, students must answer the same validated questions. If every student’s lessons and questions differ, results can’t be compared, which is why our graded checks stay fixed.

Costs move with prices

Gemini 3.8 Flash doubles in price on 1 January 2027. Per-student generation pays every price change for every new student; a library paid once is unaffected.

Every syllabus change multiplies

When a board changes a chapter, per-student generation rewrites it for every student. A library rewrites it once per interest.

Storage is not a problem: about 1.6 MB per student for one format, 4.6 MB for three.

Recommendation

Keep the shared library, and spend on what students feel

  • Personal where it is cheap: the per-student layer (their own hobby story, chat, practice) already costs about ₹3.79 a month and is checked by a second AI.
  • Add interests, not per-student lessons: fifty interests across the whole syllabus in three formats is about ₹3 lakh, once, on the strongest model. That buys far more personalisation than generating for 150 individual students.
  • Write on first request, then reuse: generate a lesson for a new concept and interest the first time any student needs it, check it, and serve it to everyone after. You only pay for what is used, and every lesson can still be reviewed.
  • Spend the savings on people: a physics teacher reading every chapter is the cost that matters most for trust, and a library makes it possible.

Limits of these numbers

  • Token counts for per-student lessons are estimates built from measured lesson and prompt sizes, not from a run of lessons generated per student. Treat them as the right size, not exact.
  • “Thinking” tokens are assumed equal to the lesson length; they vary by model and could be lower or higher.
  • Prompt caching could cut the input cost of the writer’s instructions; input is under half the cost on most models here, so the conclusions hold.
  • Images are excluded: we assume each lesson reuses shared illustrations, as EcoLearn does today.
  • The live-extras figure comes from a small number of real runs (6 stories, 4 plans, 1 coaching run). The doubt-chat figure inside it is an estimate.
  • Prices are Google’s list prices checked 13 October 2026; check again before quoting. Rupee figures use ₹88 per dollar.
